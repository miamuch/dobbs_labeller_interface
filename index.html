Task

Label the stance a human reader would infer from the tweet.

Read only the tweet shown on the labeling screen. Do not try to infer what the model, topic model, author network, or dataset construction process would say. The goal is a human gold label for stance.

Required Fields

  • Human stance: choose the best stance label for the tweet.
  • Confidence: rate how confident you are in that stance label.
  • Narrative frame: choose the main argument, story, or framing device used by the tweet, if one is clear.
  • Words that justify your label: copy a short phrase from the tweet that made you choose the stance.
  • Unusable tweet: mark yes only when the tweet should not be labeled because the text itself is unusable.

Label Meanings

Pro-choice
Supports abortion rights, reproductive freedom, abortion as healthcare, opposition to abortion bans, or criticism of anti-abortion actors/policies.
Pro-life
Opposes abortion, supports abortion bans/restrictions, emphasizes unborn life/personhood, or criticizes abortion-rights actors/policies from an anti-abortion position.
Neutral/news
Reports or summarizes events without taking a clear side, including headlines, procedural updates, or informational statements.
None/unclear
Abortion stance cannot be determined, the tweet is off-topic, too ambiguous, too truncated, or uses relevant words without a clear position.

Narrative Frame

  • Choose the frame that best describes how the tweet talks about abortion, politics, rights, law, medicine, morality, or related issues.
  • Use Unclear or multiple frames when several frames are equally central.
  • Use Not about abortion when the tweet does not actually frame abortion or abortion politics.

Confidence

  • High: the stance is explicit or nearly unmistakable.
  • Medium: the stance is likely but requires some interpretation.
  • Low: the tweet is ambiguous; choose the best label but explain briefly in notes.

Exclusion Rules

  • Use yes for spam/merch, non-English tweets, duplicates that should be dropped, unusably truncated text, or unrelated content.
  • Use no for ordinary political disagreement, sarcasm, quoting, retweets, or news items that can still be labeled.
  • If excluding, add a short reason such as non-English, too truncated, duplicate, unclear, or spam/merch.
